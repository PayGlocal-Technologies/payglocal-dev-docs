PayGlocal supports two ways to accept payments. The right one depends on a single question: **do you want to manage payments from a dashboard, or from your own website/app?**

---

## No-Code Integration

**Best for:** Businesses that want to start collecting payments immediately — no developers needed.

With No-Code, you create and manage everything from the **GCC dashboard**. No API calls, no backend setup. PayGlocal hosts the payment experience for you.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Payment Link" icon="link" color="#10B981">
    Generate a URL and share it over email, SMS, or WhatsApp. Customer pays on a PayGlocal-hosted page.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Payment Invoice Link" icon="file-invoice" color="#10B981">
    Request payment against a specific invoice. Same hosted checkout, invoice-style framing.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Payment Button" icon="computer-mouse" color="#10B981">
    Embed a button on your website. Customer clicks and lands on PayGlocal-hosted checkout.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**When to choose this:**
- You want to collect payments without touching code
- You manage transactions and reports entirely from the dashboard
- You don't have a dedicated checkout flow on your website

<Card title="Explore No-Code Products →" icon="sparkles" href="/no-code/overview">
  Payment Link, Invoice Link, and Payment Button — all dashboard-driven.
</Card>

## API Integration

**Best for:** Businesses with a website or app that has its own checkout flow and wants payments embedded directly into it.

With API integration, your backend calls PayGlocal's APIs. You control the payment experience; PayGlocal handles the processing, 3DS authentication, and security.

**When to choose this:**
- You have a dedicated checkout or cart flow on your website or app
- You want payments to feel native to your product
- You need programmatic control over transaction logic

## PayCollect vs PayDirect

PayGlocal offers two API integration modes. The single question that decides which one you need:
**Who collects the card details — you or PayGlocal?**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="PayCollect" icon="window" color="#1A6FE8">
    **PayGlocal collects card details.**

    You send a payment request. PayGlocal redirects the customer to a secure hosted checkout. You never touch card data.

    → No PCI DSS certification needed.

    - Hosted secure payment page
    - Built-in 3DS authentication
    - No PCI DSS compliance required
  </Card>
  <Card title="PayDirect" icon="code" color="#8B5CF6">
    **You collect card details.**

    Your frontend has its own payment form. Your backend sends the card data directly to PayGlocal. No redirect ever happens.

    → Requires PCI DSS Level 1 certification.

    - Custom UI integration
    - Full control over UX
    - Merchant based payment flows
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### PayCollect — Hosted Payment Page

**What is PayCollect?**

PayCollect is a **hosted payment page** solution where PayGlocal manages the entire collection experience. Customers are redirected to a **secure PayGlocal page** to enter card details, which significantly reduces your security and compliance obligations compared to collecting card data on your own domain.

**Payment Flow**

1. **Merchant initiates** — Your backend calls the PayCollect API
2. **PayGlocal hosted page** — Customer is redirected to PayGlocal's secure checkout
3. **Customer enters details** — Card information collected on PayGlocal's interface
4. **Payment processed** — PayGlocal handles 3DS authentication and processing
5. **Merchant callback** — Result sent to your callback URL

**Key Benefits**

- **Lower PCI scope** — Card data captured on PayGlocal-hosted surfaces
- **Faster go-live** — Redirect-based integration with fewer moving parts
- **Operational clarity** — Clear handoff between your order system and checkout
- **Consistent payer UX** — Hosted page optimized for completion and 3DS

No card data in the payload — PayGlocal collects it on the hosted page. PayGlocal returns a `redirectUrl`; send the customer there to complete payment.

**Supported Payment Methods:** Cards, Apple Pay, UPI, Net Banking and other Alternative Payment sections

---

### PayDirect — Direct Integration

**What is PayDirect?**

PayGlocal **PayDirect** is a **direct payment integration** for **PCI DSS compliant** merchants who collect card data on **their own interface**. You maintain complete control of the payment flow and customer experience while PayGlocal processes securely through the gateway. Suited for enterprises that need maximum customization and direct card handling.

**Payment Flow**

1. **Merchant interface** — Customer enters card details on your checkout page
2. **Card data collection** — Your frontend captures card information
3. **Submit to PayGlocal** — Your backend sends card data via API
4. **PayGlocal gateway** — Processes the payment securely
5. **Payment processed** — Result returned to your system

**Key Benefits**

- **Full UI customization** — Own the entire checkout layout and validation UX
- **Direct processing** — Submit payment instructions from your controlled environment
- **Advanced API access** — Compose richer flows when your MID supports them
- **Real-time analytics** — Tie gateway responses back to your order and risk stack

Card data collected on your own UI and sent directly. No redirect. Payment processed on your domain.

## Not Sure? Use Both.

No-Code and API integration are not mutually exclusive. Many merchants use Payment Links for quick ad-hoc collections while their checkout uses PayCollect or PayDirect for their core flow.

## Quick Decision Guide

```
How do you want to accept payments?
│
├─ From the GCC dashboard only (no code)
│   └─ → No-Code Integration
│         (Payment Link / Invoice Link / Payment Button)
│
└─ From your own website or app
    │
    ├─ Are you PCI DSS certified?
    │   │
    │   ├─ NO  → PayCollect
    │   │         PayGlocal hosts the payment page.
    │   │
    │   └─ YES → PayDirect
    │             You collect card data on your own page.
    │
    └─ Need both? Use No-Code for ad-hoc + API for checkout.
```
